About

Literature as a way of thinking across borders.

I am Professor of Comparative Literature at the University of Santiago de Compostela, where my work has developed at the intersection of comparative literature, world literature and translation studies.

My research is concerned with the movement of texts between languages, cultures and literary systems, and with the forms of mediation that make those movements possible. This has led me to work on literary circulation, translation, cultural memory, postcolonial questions and the changing geographies of world literature.

In recent years, I have increasingly explored the environmental, spatial and public dimensions of literary research. My current interests include literary cartography, relations between literature and territory, environmental humanities and forms of applied humanities that bring scholarship into dialogue with cultural institutions, digital environments and local communities.

My academic work has been shaped by sustained international collaboration. I have held visiting and research positions at the University of Glasgow and participate in international institutions and networks devoted to comparative literature, world literature and literary history, including Academia Europaea, the Harvard Institute for World Literature and the Stockholm Collegium of World Literary History.

Research

Comparative research across texts, territories and cultural systems.

My work combines comparative literary analysis with questions of circulation, mediation, translation and space. Rather than treating literature as a collection of self-contained national traditions, I am interested in the relations through which texts acquire meaning, mobility and new cultural lives.
