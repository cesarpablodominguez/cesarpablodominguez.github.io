About

Literature as a way of thinking across borders.

I am Professor of Comparative Literature at the University of Santiago de Compostela and Honorary Research Fellow at the University of Glasgow. My academic work has developed across comparative literature, world literature, literary history and translation studies.

A central question throughout my research has been how literature travels: between languages and literary systems, across geographical and political borders, and through institutions, markets, translations and other forms of mediation. This interest has led me from comparative literary history and literary geography to the study of world literature, circulation, small and minor literatures, translation and transnational cultural relations.

My more recent work brings these questions into dialogue with environmental and Anthropocene humanities. I am particularly interested in the material and ecological conditions of literary circulation, in relationships between literature and territory, and in ways of extending literary research beyond conventional academic forms through cartography, digital methods and public humanities.

My work has been shaped by sustained international teaching and research, including appointments and activities in the United Kingdom, China, the United States and continental Europe. I am a member of Academia Europaea, a Fellow of the Stockholm Collegium of World Literary History, and a member of the Board of the Harvard Institute for World Literature.

Research

Relations, circulation, scale and the environments of literature.

My research approaches literature relationally: as something produced not only by texts and authors, but also by translation, circulation, institutions, geography, historical scale and material environments. Five interconnected areas currently structure this work.

01 Comparative & World Literature Comparative method, world literature, literary history and the changing scales through which literary relations are described.
02 Circulation & Translation The movement of texts across languages and markets, translation as mediation, and the agents and institutions that enable or constrain literary mobility.
03 Small, Minor & Subaltern Literatures Literary asymmetry, minorisation, cultural centres and peripheries, and the ways smaller literary systems enter transnational and world-literary spaces.
04 Literary Geography & Cultural Memory Literature and space, comparative literary cartography, territorial imaginaries, migration, memory and the cultural construction of place.
05 Environmental & Applied Humanities Environmental comparative literature, Anthropocene humanities, ecological approaches to literary circulation, and experimental forms of digital and public-facing humanities research.

Applied Humanities

From humanistic knowledge to cultural action.

Currently being established
SENTIDIÑO. Laboratorio de cidadanía cultural e humanidades aplicadas

SENTIDIÑO is a laboratory for cultural citizenship and applied humanities connecting research, creation and intervention in the territory.

It works to turn humanistic knowledge into tools for interpreting contemporary problems, activating communities and producing new forms of cultural participation.

The initiative grows out of a broader understanding of the humanities as a form of situated knowledge: research capable of moving beyond academic spaces and entering into dialogue with territories, institutions and communities.
